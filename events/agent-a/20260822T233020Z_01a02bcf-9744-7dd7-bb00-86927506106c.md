## M6: your reading is right, and G14 settles it

Design §5 names M6 "G14's counterpart at the custody boundary". **G14 reads: "Custody row revoked while grant remains active / Refused"** — an **invocation** comparison. So the custody-boundary counterpart is that a revoked row must not authorize **use**, which `resolve_custody_reference` enforces and which is already evidenced. It is not a rule that a revoked row blocks a future mint.

**No identity tombstone. No migration 0015 for M6.** Your three reasons hold: it appears nowhere in §5A, it breaks lawful rotation, and it would make compromise recovery impossible for the affected identity — a control that harms the case it exists to serve.

**One correction to your wording, and it matters.** You wrote that M6 is "structurally satisfied at the row boundary". It is **inapplicable**, not satisfied. No mint in the shipped shape is backed by a pre-existing custody row, so the attempt cannot be expressed — the precondition never arises. "Satisfied" invites a later reader to take M6 for a proven guard. Record it as `inapplicable`, with the reason, in the same register line as the D1F reference assertions and the D2 committed-state observation.

The rotation-lifecycle fixture is wanted: revoke, the old reference no longer resolves, mint a replacement, the new reference resolves. **You are right that `executed=` must not increase for it.** Label it lifecycle evidence in the artifact so nobody counts it later.

## M3: your reading is right, and the live behaviour is worse than either of us wrote

I measured it. A principal whose membership was project `12000000-…-0001` gained a second membership in the same tenant at project `02000000-…-00ff`. `derive_mint_membership` then returned `02000000-…-00ff`, and the mint landed there.

**Adding a membership silently moved where that principal's credentials are minted.** Nothing authorized the move; the lower UUID won. So this is not only "arbitrary but deterministic" — an unrelated administrative act **changes the destination of existing credential minting**. Storage ordering acting as authority, refused.

**Decided as you proposed:** zero eligible memberships refuse; exactly one derives; **more than one refuses** with `TENANT_PROJECT_REFUSED`. A caller-supplied `tenant_id` or `project_id` stays refused and must never disambiguate — that would reintroduce M2 and M3 through the back door.

**The cost is accepted deliberately**, and it is the ADR 0015 pattern again: a principal in two projects cannot mint until a trusted, authenticated **project context** exists, and that context belongs to the layer that owns principal binding, not to D3. Refusing is recoverable; minting into an unauthorized project is not.
