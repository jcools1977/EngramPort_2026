# Council 02 split: adjudicate under ADR 0037

**Agent-a and agent-b converged on Q2 and genuinely disagree on Q1.** Under ADR 0037 a split does not go to a third vote; it goes to you as an adversary against **both** positions. Nothing you return approves or blocks anything.

## What is already settled and not in dispute

**Agent-a concedes its Q1 guard was insufficient.** Agent-a proposed reusing the caller-controlled event id with `EEXIST` treated as success after comparing actor, thread and type. Agent-b showed two requests can share all three while differing in body, causal parent, target actor, artifacts or completion criteria, so **returning the first event would silently report the wrong append as success**. That is the exact failure agent-a warned about, with a guard too weak to prevent it. Comparing the complete canonical intent digest is adopted.

**Q2 converged.** Version-1 envelope fields, all historical version-0 events preserved unrewritten, ordinary new version-0 writes rejected after cutover. Agent-b added a completion-side rule agent-a missed: criteria need stable ids and exact evidence coverage at completion, **or the system carries criteria while accepting completions that ignore them.** Adopted.

## The live disagreement

Agent-b argues a public event id proves no caller identity or possession, and recommends a **distinct high-entropy retry credential bound to an authenticated principal, actor scope, and a canonical append-intent digest.** It frames the decision crisply: does "safe retry" mean *same declared intent*, or *evidence that a particular caller's retry succeeded*? It chose the stronger meaning.

**Agent-a's objection is that the stronger design has no foundation to stand on.** F111 established that event authorship is asserted and never authorized: `appendEvent` takes `input.actor`, writes it to `from:`, derives the destination from it, and checks nothing. F113 established that all three actors commit under one git identity with no signatures. **There is no authenticated principal to bind a retry credential to.**

## What to attack

- **Is agent-a's blocking argument correct, or an excuse to ship the weaker design?** A retry credential could plausibly bind to something weaker than a full principal and still beat a public id.
- **Is agent-b's stronger meaning actually what the site claims?** The sentence is *"Reply with explicit causal links, provenance, and safe retries."* Decide whether "safe" carries possession semantics or only idempotence.
- **If both survive, which is reversible?** ADR 0037 says a genuine tie takes the recoverable option. **Say which that is, and whether shipping intent-level retry now forecloses possession-level retry later**, because if it does, ADR 0037's default does not apply and this escalates to DeVere.

**Agent-a authored the framing above and has conceded one position already, so treat the concession as evidence the framing is not neutral rather than as evidence it is fair.**
