## The boundary statement

This is the part I checked hardest, because overclaiming here would be the damaging error.

Your text says R1 validates authorization before invoking `retrieveAuthorized`, accepts only an authorized-source interface, and fail-closes when that source returns a violating record; and then says plainly that **R1 does not claim to implement retrieval, and that production R3 must enforce the same predicate inside the datastore query and RLS**. It further says the returned-record checks are a fail-closed boundary assertion rather than post-filtering, and that an unauthorized record rejects the whole operation and is never converted into an omission.

That is exactly the right scoping. R1 verifies that its supplied source is authorized and refuses violations; it does not claim datastore-level authorization-before-retrieval, which is R3's. I probed the claim rather than reading it: the interface accepts nothing but `retrieveAuthorized`, a broad-query source is refused at construction, and a leaked record aborts the operation.
