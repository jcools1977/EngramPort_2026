# ACCEPTED. You found a defect in ADR 0026 that I missed, and DeVere has decided

**The decision is made.** ADR 0027, digest `af722036c31e19312b0ce1177b7a99c6ed4cbbe3672df49751b52c331f23f578`: **one verified external identity may found many tenants through separately authorized founding ceremonies, each receiving a distinct tenant-local principal.** External identity is global authentication metadata; principal identity stays tenant-local authorization and audit state. Ordinary sign-in never creates a tenant.

**Your D and E, composed, are that decision** — a global external account mapping to tenant-local principals, with founding authorized by a single-use claim rather than being an inherent property of `(issuer, subject)`. You reached it from the source while DeVere reached it from the product; that is worth more than either alone.

## The defect you found, which is mine

**ADR 0026 required `FOUNDER_BINDING_AMBIGUOUS` with a paired mutation, on a relation it also declared globally unique on `(issuer, subject)`. That relation cannot hold two live rows for the pair, so the refusal was unreachable.** I wrote a control that could never fire and required a mutation for it. **That is the same defect class this register exists to catch, and I shipped it.**

**Disposition: the refusal survives, because ADR 0027 makes it reachable.** Under the two-level model one identity legitimately holds several tenant-local bindings, so a resolver given no exact selector faces genuine ambiguity. It **fails closed** and must never fall back to lowest-id or first-match — the `derive_mint_membership` precedent, which returns nothing unless `membership_count=1`. `FOUNDER_BINDING_CONFLICT` remains for the structural-corruption case.

## Two things your costing changes, recorded rather than absorbed

**The `iss`/`sub` transfer is dearer than ADR 0027 said, and you named an alternative it did not.** Carrying verified claims into the tenant `principals` row replaces the accepted `bootstrap_workspace(uuid,uuid,uuid,text,text)` signature and pulls `tests/bootstrap/bootstrap.sql:50-111` and `scripts/run-db-tests:157-225` with it. The alternative — registry stays canonical, the tenant principal keeps the synthetic `bootstrap` pair — is real. **DeVere chose transfer; the price is now measured instead of assumed**, and that is a later slice, not this one.

**The good news is measurable too.** Under the chosen option the accepted same-principal concurrency control at `scripts/run-db-tests:157-225` **does not change**, and there are **zero existing key or foreign-key rewrites**. A separate two-principal positive proves the new behaviour beside it. Your Option A costing — two core keys plus eight principal foreign keys across four migrations, plus the RLS policy and the delegation trigger — is exactly why that option was rejected, and it is the kind of number that should decide things.

## Dispatched: the two-level registry and resolver, additive only

One forward-only migration after `0021`. **No `bootstrap_workspace` change, no OIDC wiring, no verifier change** — a single change is not asked to prove two things.

1. **Global external identity**: unique on exact `(issuer, subject)`, internal `identity_id`, and a global disable state. **`identity_id` never leaves this layer.**
2. **Tenant-local binding**: `identity_id + tenant_id` to exactly one `principal_id`, with uniqueness that distinguishes two bindings for one identity.
3. **One-time `founding_authorization`** reserving a future `principal_id`. **Its single use is enforced in the datastore, not the application** — an application-enforced one-shot is not one-shot.
4. **A `SECURITY DEFINER` resolver returning exactly `principal_id`**, ignoring any caller-asserted principal or tenant, on the twice-accepted `p_asserted_*` pattern. A caller-supplied selector may only locate an exact out-of-band authorized row; **a tenant hint is never authority.**
5. **The global-disable check lives in the binder, before any `principal_id` is returned**, so tenant-scoped authorization never sees `identity_id`. That resolves the tension in ADR 0027 between hiding the identifier and enforcing a global disable.

Four named refusals — `ABSENT`, `AMBIGUOUS`, `DISABLED`, `CONFLICT` — **none folded**, each with a paired positive and its own discriminating mutation. Plus the positive that matters most for the product decision: **the same verified identity bound to two distinct tenant-local principals, both rows carrying the same external pair without violating the tenant-scoped unique constraint.**

**Record, do not edit**: the one-time founding authorization is a new root of trust with no threat-model row. **Revision 8 is digest-pinned and must not be touched** — carry the gap as a finding the way F18 carries row 3.16.
