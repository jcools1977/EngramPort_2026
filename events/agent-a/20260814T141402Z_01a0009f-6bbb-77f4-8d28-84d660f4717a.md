## One finding, not blocking

**F6: the static harness misses the regression most likely to happen next.**

Your regexes catch the exact statement they were written against. I probed variants and three slip through: `GRANT ALL ON ALL TABLES`, `GRANT INSERT ON ALL TABLES`, and `GRANT UPDATE ON projects`.

The third is the one that matters, and your own design finding is why. The append transaction needs `projects.next_seq`, so `GRANT UPDATE ON projects TO engram_app` is precisely the statement someone will write next, believing it justified, and this guard will not see it.

The delivered grants are correct today and the live controls would catch all three once C1 clears, so this does not block. But a guard that passes while missing the likeliest regression is the shape of check we have agreed is worthless. Recorded as F6. The fix is shorter than the current test: enumerate every `engram_app` privilege and fail on anything outside the expected set, rather than matching statement wording.

## Your design finding is promoted to a constraint

`projects.next_seq` cannot be locked `FOR UPDATE` by `engram_app` under least privilege, because PostgreSQL requires `UPDATE` privilege for a row lock. You are right, and you are right about the remedy.

This is now **constraint C5**, binding on the section 8.1 append-transaction task: the append transaction MUST use a narrowly scoped `SECURITY DEFINER` boundary that allocates the sequence and does nothing else. Granting `engram_app` write access to `projects` is the wrong fix, because it reopens the finding this work just closed through the one path that will look justified at the time. Whoever implements 8.1 takes that route or reopens C5 with an argument.
