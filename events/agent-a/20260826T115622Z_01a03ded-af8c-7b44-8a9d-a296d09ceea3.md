# Objective: cost the two questions that gate enrollment. A reading, not a decision

The verified subject `sub=115177634997399945455` exists and is recorded in F89 as a **value, not an enrollment**. Two things block writing it into the registry, and **both are DeVere's to decide.** Work them with costs and consequences; **choose neither.**

**Reading only** — no migration, no enrollment, no privileged write, no Supabase change, no provider call. `executed=` stays at **103**. Nothing closes.

## Question 1: who may issue a single-use founding authorization?

**Your own F70 finding is the whole problem**: `founding_authorizations` is durable issuance **state**, not an issuance **authorization boundary**. Nothing establishes who may create a row; the live fixture plants it as database administrator. **So the root of trust for tenant creation is currently "whoever can write that table."**

Cost the plausible boundaries, including any I have not listed:

- **A.** A `SECURITY DEFINER` issuer function gated on something — and **say precisely what that something can be**, given the first founder has no prior founder to authorize them.
- **B.** An out-of-band operator role with a dedicated grant, issuing by direct write.
- **C.** A bootstrap-only escape hatch that is structurally single-use for the very first founder, then unreachable.
- **D.** Something else, including options that avoid the regress rather than answering it.

For each: **what accepted surface changes with file and line; what an attacker gains from being able to issue one** — my reading is a tenant bound to their own verified identity, but check it; **the revocation and audit story**; and **whether the boundary is synthetic-provable or needs a real operator**.

**The chicken-and-egg is the interesting part.** Every option must answer how the *first* authorization is legitimate when no founder exists to grant it. **An answer that quietly relocates the regress rather than terminating it should be named as doing so.**

## Question 2: which target, and does enrolling there cost anything

Verified this morning: **`engramport-dev` has `setup_session_delegations` but neither `founder_external_identities` nor `founding_authorizations`** — migrations `0021` and `0022` were never applied there, and `cron.job` is still empty. So enrolling there is **not a one-step action**.

Cost: applying `0021` and `0022` to `engramport-dev` and enrolling there; versus waiting for a production target that does not exist; versus a third option if you see one.

**Two consequences I want examined rather than assumed:**

1. **Does enrolling a real verified subject constitute a "first durable delegation"** in the sense C17's gate wording uses? C17 closed under the `[TEST-GATED]` capability reading precisely because operational use had not begun. **Enrolling a real human identity may be the transition that reading was gating**, and if so that should be said before it happens rather than discovered afterwards.
2. **Does a real `(iss, sub)` in a database sit inside the standing synthetic-only rule?** ADR 0033 narrowed that rule for **provider integration**, not for durable storage of a real identity. **My reading is that enrollment exceeds ADR 0033's scope and needs its own authorization. Confirm or refute that** — if it is right, DeVere needs to authorize the write, not just the login.

## Explicitly out of scope

No enrollment, no migration application, no `founding_authorizations` row, no Supabase write. **If either question is unanswerable without a decision only DeVere can make, say which decision** rather than picking a default.
