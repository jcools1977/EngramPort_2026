# Independent review of delivered work, not of a dispatch

**This is a departure from your usual target and the schema is an imperfect fit. Use `findings` for what matters and treat `dispatch_feasibility` as a verdict on whether the delivered change is sound.**

**Why you are being asked.** Until now agent-a has been the only check on agent-b's implementation, and agent-a's specifications have repeatedly been the defective half: F102 and F103 were agent-a's own errors, discovered only after agent-a had already accepted the work they concerned. **Nobody reviews agent-a's acceptances.** That is the hole.

## What was delivered

`AGENTS.md` rule 5 was split, per DeVere's approval in ADR 0038, into actor-owned append-only collaboration history and shared editable implementation surfaces. Rule 7's ban changed from overwriting "another actor's file" to "another actor's accepted event or referenced artifact". A new control, `tests/repository-surface-policy.test.mjs`, enumerates every tracked path and asserts the written rules account for all of them.

## What agent-a already verified, so do not spend the review re-deriving it

- The control passes now: `tracked=750 actor_rule=true shared_rule=true broad_ban=false unaccounted=0`.
- **It genuinely discriminates.** Agent-a restored the former broad wording by hand and the control failed with `shared_rule=false unaccounted=237`. It would have rejected this repository before the amendment.

## What to attack

**The question agent-a cannot answer about its own review is whether the split quietly removed a protection.** Rule 5's broad wording forbade an actor creating files anywhere outside its own prefixes. That ban is now gone for source, tests, docs, configuration and migrations.

- **Does any security property in this project depend on the old breadth rather than on the ownership rule that replaced it?** Agent-b asserts append enforces registered-directory ownership, verification enforces filename identity and registered paths, content hashes are verified, and artifact references must sit in the author's prefix and match their digest. **Check whether those four actually cover what the broad ban covered**, or whether something fell between them.
- **Rule 7 narrowed from "another actor's file" to "another actor's accepted event or referenced artifact."** What was protected by the wider wording that is now unprotected? Migrations, `actors/` records and test fixtures are all now shared editable surfaces.
- **Can one actor now modify `actors/*.yaml`?** That file defines who may write where, and the policy control reads it to decide what is permitted. **A rule that derives its authority from a file the rule permits editing is circular.** Say whether that circularity is real here.
- Is the control itself falsifiable in the way agent-a claimed, or does `unaccounted=0` hold trivially because the shared rule admits every path outside two directories?

**Do not soften a finding because agent-a verified part of this already.** Agent-a's verification is the thing under review.
