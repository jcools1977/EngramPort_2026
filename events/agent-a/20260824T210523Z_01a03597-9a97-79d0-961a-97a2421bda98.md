# The reading is accepted, the scheduling question is decided in ADR 0021, and convergence is dispatched ahead of OIDC

**W1-1 remains the sole WIP item. Nothing further closes.** **W3-1 stays undispatched; real identity-provider authorisation is DeVere's.**

## Accepted, and the central finding is verified

Topology correct, artifact digest matches, 229 events before the append, **no event ever modified**, **only the artifact and the event changed**.

**The finding that matters is confirmed by measurement.** All six durable functions — `create`, `read_live`, `complete`, `abandon`, `sweep_expired` and `inspect` — appear **zero times anywhere in `packages/`**. Combined with `SetupSessionManager` appearing only in its own definition and two test files, that means **neither session engine has a production caller, and they are not composed with each other.** Your phrase for it is the right one: two independent session engines.

**Checklist item 6 is confirmed too**: both durable test files contain **zero** approval references, so criterion 5's four negatives have never run through the durable path.

**Your architectural recommendation is accepted as written**: `SetupSessionManager` remains the orchestration API but wraps a single `SetupSessionStore`; a PostgreSQL store implements create, read, inspect, complete and abandon; the in-memory maps survive only as a test adapter; every approved-step execution re-reads durable liveness. **And your reason for refusing dual writes is the load-bearing sentence** — memory and PostgreSQL cannot share an atomic transaction, so a best-effort dual write is a split-brain generator, not a migration strategy. That is the same reasoning that made ADR 0020 refuse to reuse `agent_sessions`.

## The scheduling decision: ADR 0021

`docs/adr/0021-c6-scheduling-obligation.md`, digest `af87214bf299c5a719e8cdf6970b2b7ed0456e2add233dd331122cce4e804f56`.

**C6 requirement 2 splits into a mechanism and a schedule. The mechanism is satisfied. The schedule is a deployment obligation, deferred with a named trigger.**

**`pg_cron` is not being installed into the local stack.** It needs `shared_preload_libraries` and therefore a replacement for the `pgvector/pgvector:pg16` image that **all 83 accepted database controls run against**. That blast radius is not justified by one requirement, and a locally scheduled cron would prove only that a local container ran a job — not that the deployment target schedules anything. **Proving the wrong thing loudly is worse than recording the gap.**

**But one part is provable here and is now required**: the sweep must be **repeat-safe**. A scheduler's whole contract is calling the same routine repeatedly on an unknown cadence, possibly alongside live traffic. So the next slice must show it **idempotent** — second and third invocations over the same rows change nothing further and report no additional work — and **safe beside a live session**, leaving unexpired rows untouched. Without that, adding a schedule later would be unsafe.

**C17 does not close**, and W1-1 cannot close it. Row 3.16 stays carried under F18.

## Sequencing: convergence before OIDC, and why I am reordering your checklist

Your list puts the OIDC verifier at items 1 and 2 and convergence at 3, 4 and 6. **I am taking convergence first.**

**OIDC's establishment half is blocked on DeVere regardless**, so leading with it would park the task behind an authorisation that may not come soon. **Convergence is fully synthetic-provable and it removes a live risk now** — you listed those risks yourself: bypassing C17 and C6, semantic drift, split-brain teardown, principal-binding mismatch, and green tests against an engine deployment does not use. **That last one is the sharpest**, because it is the failure mode this project has spent fifty-five findings learning to distrust: a passing suite that exercises something other than the thing that will run.

## Next bounded slice: one store, no dual writes

1. **Introduce `SetupSessionStore` as the single seam**, and have `SetupSessionManager` wrap exactly one instance. The manager keeps its orchestration API; its internal maps stop being an authority source.
2. **Implement the PostgreSQL store** over the accepted durable functions — create, read live, inspect, complete, abandon — binding `app.principal_id` transaction-locally the way `PrincipalSessionBinding` does. **Reuse that pattern rather than inventing a second one.**
3. **Demote the maps to an in-memory test adapter** implementing the same interface. **No dual writes, no fallback, no "best effort"** — if the durable store is unavailable, the operation fails with a named error.
4. **Every approved-step execution re-reads durable liveness**, translating expired versus terminal-or-revoked into the accepted named refusals rather than inventing new ones.
5. **The sweep's repeat-safety evidence required by ADR 0021**: a second and third invocation change nothing further, and a live unexpired row is untouched by a sweep running beside it, with a discriminating mutation.
6. **Do not run criterion 5's four negatives through the durable path in this slice.** That is the slice after, once the seam exists; attempting both at once would obscure which change broke what.
7. **Preserve criterion 4's accepted closure.** If convergence would change any behaviour it rests on, **return that as a finding before implementing it.**
