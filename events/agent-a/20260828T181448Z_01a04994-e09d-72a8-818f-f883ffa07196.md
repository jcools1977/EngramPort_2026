# Council 04 result: convergence, a synthesis, and one decision escalated

**Reported to DeVere for verification under ADR 0035. Nothing here is adopted repository state.**

## The mechanism worked for the first time

Agent-a sealed `f6ed622b...` in `7822491` at 09:00 with the plaintext held **outside the repository**; agent-b sealed `e10e6708...` in `811a1e1` at 11:21. Both digests were committed before either plaintext existed anywhere agent-b could reach. Both revealed files reproduce their digests exactly.

**Independence was a property of availability rather than of attestation**, which is what ADR 0041 was written for and what the previous four councils could not claim.

## Convergence

Disposition must be durable and reconstructible on a fresh clone while the durable-cursor claim stands; a local cursor is only a disposable cache; an observation is a notification and grants no turn, claim, approval or implementation authority; disposition must not use `in_reply_to`, which would consume strict relay's single reply slot; one record per observation has unacceptable growth, so records acknowledge ordered ranges; at-least-once is sufficient, so a crash may duplicate but must not silently lose an uncheckpointed range.

**That is enough to reject both a mutable shared projection and local-only state.**

## The split, and why it was a synthesis rather than a tie

Agent-a proposed the **subscriber** appends a batched `acknowledgment` in its own directory. Agent-b proposed an access-controlled **service** stream written by EngramPort.

Agent-b's reading is that these are not competing storage algorithms but **two different acceptance boundaries**: a subscriber assertion is the strongest evidence of acceptance the protocol can represent, while a service assertion proves product delivery but not semantic attention. **Neither can prove a person or model understood anything**, and the returned contract uses subscriber assertion where the subscriber can write it, permitting a service record only where the declared boundary is itself service-controlled, and never impersonating the subscriber.

**Agent-b also designed the part agent-a left as a note.** Agent-a wrote that batching "should be stated as part of the design" and did not design it; agent-b specified cumulative checkpoints bound to subscription id, selector revision, covered range, batch digest and count, and prior checkpoint id, with rebuild by verifying the checkpoint chain.

## Agent-a's classification was wrong

**Agent-a called this ADR 0028 class two because `acknowledgment` already exists.** That reasoning is syntactic: reusing an envelope avoids a schema addition, it does not avoid a product decision. The behavior exposes durable subscriber viewing history, defines who may create and read those records, introduces caller-visible replay and retry semantics, and decides how selector changes affect old disposition. **ADR 0028 places privacy, authorization, retry behavior and caller-visible workflow in class three.** Conceded.

## For DeVere

**Is observation disposition project-visible actor history, or private subscription history?** If project-visible, the actor-owned event directory is the simplest canonical location. If not, an access-controlled control stream is required. In both cases the mutable cursor remains a projection of append-only accepted records.
