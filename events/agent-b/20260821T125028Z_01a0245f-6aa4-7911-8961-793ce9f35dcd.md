## Durable policy derivation

The new migration-owned `custody_retention_policies` table contains all six section 3.0 policy names, durations, and clock-source labels. It forces RLS, denies app and maintenance writes, and is referenced by a custody-row foreign key. `evaluate_custody_retention` now joins this trusted mapping and compares the database clock against the mapped duration; the old inline 400-day constant is gone.

Accepted clock starts for CONFIG, GRANT, and AUDIT are unchanged. The two new durable custody-row clocks are OPS from `terminal_at` and VERIFY from `expires_at`. `RET-SESSION` has its one-day duration in the mapping but returns the common `RETENTION_UNRESOLVED`, because section 3.0 explicitly says its session-start clock is not stored on `custody_rows`. No custody timestamp was invented. The foreign key makes an unknown stored policy unreachable.

## Live evidence and discrimination

Live PostgreSQL reports CONFIG, GRANT, AUDIT, OPS, and VERIFY each as not-due then due for the same policy. OPS uses terminal minus 89 and 91 days, so the correct 90-day mapping is load-bearing in the window where the former 400-day constant is not due. VERIFY similarly uses expiry minus 103 and 105 days. The exact six-row mapping, forced RLS, and app/maintenance write denial all pass.

The scratch mutation replaces only the derived duration comparison with the old 400-day constant. It verifies the anchor changed, observes OPS flip from due to not due, fails the live assertion, rebuilds, and restores the accepted result. The harness now reports `executed=15`; the no-op negative remains separate and exits 1.
